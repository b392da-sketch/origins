# Rival Civilisation Simulator — Design Draft v0.6

**Status:** draft for review. Nothing has been coded. Every section is open to change; items marked **PROPOSED** are suggestions awaiting your decision.
**Date:** 9 November 2026 (v0.6: additions agreed in principle, section 16; v0.5: the world begins with three bands of 30)
**Owner of design decisions:** Ben. Claude proposes options and consequences; Ben approves milestone gates.

---

## 1. Vision

A self-running world that begins with a single band of people and grows, over tens of thousands of simulated years, into whatever its people make of it: villages, chiefdoms, states, empires, religions, trade networks, industry, and possibly beyond. The observer watches from above, zooms from continents down to individual people, and can always ask *why* something happened and get a true answer from causes the simulation actually computed.

The target is **plausible and legible**, not "realistic" in the sense of calibrated to real history. Different runs should produce different histories from the same rules.

Most of the effort goes into the mechanics and complexity of the simulation. The display is 2D.

## 3. Time

**Two phases with an event-based switch:**

| Phase | Clock (real time) | Switch |
|---|---|---|
| Prehistory | 300 simulated years per day | Ends when the first sustained farming settlement appears anywhere in the world |
| History | 10–25 simulated years per day | Continues indefinitely |

The switch is triggered by the world, not by a date, so it happens at a different time in each run.

**Implications to keep in mind:**

- From the 50,000-year start, prehistory at 300 years/day takes roughly four months of real time if farming appears around 12,000 years before the present (it may come earlier or later in a given run). History from the start of farming to a modern-equivalent world (on the order of 12,000 years) takes roughly 1.3–3.3 years at 10–25 years/day.
- Fast-forward, pause and replay are always available; the clock is a scheduler on top of the simulation, not part of it.
- The simulation itself steps in small internal ticks (**PROPOSED:** coarser steps such as yearly for wandering bands, seasonal for settled societies), so change appears continuous to the observer.

## 4. The world

**Globe mechanics, flat display.** The world is generated and simulated on a sphere (**PROPOSED:** a hex-like grid on an icosahedron, which avoids the distortion of a square grid near the poles). It is shown as a flat map with edges, using a map projection.

- East–west travel wraps around as on a real globe; the map's edges are a viewing convention only.
- Latitude drives sunlight, seasons and climate zones.
- Distances and areas are true on the sphere, so travel and land use are not distorted by the projection.

**Generation (Minecraft-like, random from a seed):**

- Landmasses and mountains (plate-like processes or layered noise)
- Elevation, rivers from water flow, lakes, coasts
- Climate: temperature by latitude and altitude, rainfall by winds and terrain, seasonal cycles
- Biomes from climate (forest, grassland, desert, tundra and so on)
- Resources: flint, clay, timber, copper, tin, iron, coal, salt and others, placed by geology
- **Wild species that can be domesticated, placed unevenly.** This matters a great deal: it is what makes farming possible in some regions and not others (behaviours 3 and 10).

**Vastness.** Terrain is cheap and can be generated in detail only where needed. The true limit on size is the number of people and societies being simulated, which is why the individual-to-group folding (section 5) matters.

### 4.1 World-generation proposals (PROPOSED, awaiting decision)

**Size: a smaller globe whose map grows (DECIDED in principle).** A globe has a fixed size, so the map cannot literally grow without losing globe mechanics (latitude, seasons, wrap-around). Instead:

- The planet is **smaller than Earth** (DECIDED: about a quarter of Earth's surface area, i.e. half its radius) and is fixed when the world is created, from its seed.
- Only the area people have reached is generated in detail and simulated. Everything beyond is **unknown on the map** until people explore it.
- The **known world grows** as bands spread, and later as nations, traders and explorers push outward. Each culture could also keep its own known world, like historical maps.
- When the whole planet is known, the world has been fully explored, which fits the later step "beyond the one world".

**Grid.** A geodesic hex grid. For Earth-sized reference, grid sizes follow 10·4ⁿ+2 cells:

| Grid level | Cells | Area per cell | Spacing | Role |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 6 | 40,962 | ≈12,500 km² | ≈110 km | Coarse overview, climate |
| **7 (proposed base)** | **163,842** | **≈3,100 km²** | **≈56 km** | **Simulation cells: one or a few forager bands' territory** |
| 8 | 655,362 | ≈780 km² | ≈28 km | Finer detail, generated only where people settle |

Roughly 30% land gives about 49,000 land cells at level 7 on an Earth-sized planet. On the proposed quarter-area planet with the same ~56 km cells, that becomes about 41,000 cells and roughly 12,000 land cells. Detail below the base level is generated on demand.

**Generation order.**

1. Tectonic plates → continents, ocean basins, mountain ranges, volcanic and earthquake zones
2. Elevation and erosion → rivers (by water flow), lakes, deltas, coastlines
3. Climate → temperature by latitude and altitude; rainfall from prevailing winds, ocean proximity and rain shadows; seasons
4. Biomes from temperature and rainfall (tundra, boreal forest, temperate forest, grassland, savanna, desert, tropical forest, wetlands)
5. Ecology → wild plant and animal food per biome and season, with depletion and regrowth
6. Geology → flint and stone, clay, salt, copper, tin, iron, coal, gold and others, placed by rock type and tectonics
7. **Domesticable species,** placed unevenly by biome and chance: a few regions get several, many get one or none
8. Starting location: a habitable, moderately rich region (not the world's best or worst)

**Climate variability and hazards.** Year-to-year variation, multi-decade dry and wet spells, and slow long-term change. **Glacial cycles** (ice sheets, falling and rising sea levels, land bridges opening and closing) are an optional world-creation setting, off by default and built after the core works; they cannot be switched on mid-run. Hazards occur at realistic rates driven by the environment: droughts, floods, earthquakes, eruptions, wildfires, disease outbreaks.

**Map display (DECIDED).** An equirectangular projection (a simple rectangle, easy to pan and zoom, stretched near the poles) for navigation, with an optional equal-area overview for judging true sizes.

## 5. People and resolution

**Individuals at first.** The founding people (three bands of 30) are simulated person by person: name, age, sex, kinship, health, skills, beliefs, status.

**Folding into groups.** As communities grow past a threshold (**PROPOSED:** around 150 people, or when a polity forms), ordinary people fold into weighted groups by age band × role/class × place. Group totals are exact; nobody is counted twice.

**Scale constraint (DECIDED).** A full world of foragers may reach hundreds of thousands to millions of people before farming. Individuals therefore cannot persist everywhere through prehistory. Proposal: every person is individual while world population is small (up to about 20,000); beyond that, bands become the unit (with exact age and sex counts), and only notable people and a few "followed" families stay individual. The observer can choose a band to follow closely.

**Notables stay individual.** Leaders, prophets, inventors, traders, warriors and others who matter to the story are kept as individuals, drawn *from* their groups and still counted in them. Their lives, decisions and genealogies are recorded.

## 6. The core: a subsistence ecology

The first thing that must work is the simplest: people getting enough food from the land to survive and reproduce.

- Foraging, hunting and fishing yields depend on biome, season, local depletion and recovery, and the band's tools and knowledge.
- Food is energy: people need it, store it where they can, share it by social rules, and die or fail to reproduce without it.
- Births, deaths, ageing and partnership follow from health, nutrition and age.
- Bands move seasonally, split when they grow too large or quarrel, and settle where returns allow.

Everything later (surplus, trade, specialists, states, markets) must grow out of this layer. No later system may create food, people or materials from nothing; every quantity has a recorded source.

## 7. Environment and hazards

- **Random hazards** come from the environment model, so they make geographic sense: droughts in dry or variable climates, floods on rivers and coasts, earthquakes and eruptions near plate boundaries, wildfires in dry forests, disease outbreaks with density and contact.
- **Climate varies** year to year and over centuries (dry spells, cold periods, wetter eras).
- **Outcomes are emergent.** Famine follows when a harvest failure meets too little storage, poor distribution and no way of bringing food in. An epidemic's spread follows contact, density and immunity. Neither is ever rolled as an event, so both can always be explained.

---

## 8. Technology

- Real-world techniques (fire use, cordage, pottery, domestication, irrigation, metallurgy, writing, the wheel, sailing, printing, steam and so on) are nodes with **prerequisites**: materials, prior knowledge, and enough people to sustain the skill.
- **No timetable.** The chance of a technique emerging depends on need (pressure the people actually face), available materials, population size and connections, and chance.
- Techniques **spread** through contact, trade and migration, and **can be lost** when populations shrink or become isolated.
- **After the modern equivalent:** the world carries on with what exists. Speculative and science-fiction technology (spaceships, faster-than-light travel, energy weapons) is parked for a later decision (section 14).

## 9. Belief, religion and the god

**Religion arises without the god.** People interpret what happens to them (a drought, an eclipse, a good harvest, a death) through their beliefs. Beliefs spread, change, split and persist. Shared beliefs make cooperation possible beyond kinship and shape legitimacy and conflict (behaviour 9). The god's presence must never be required for religion to emerge.

**Practices have real costs.** Prayer, sacrifice, festivals, temples and priesthoods consume real food, animals, labour and materials, and so compete with other uses.

**The god (optional mode).** When enabled, the observer can act in four kinds of ways:

| Kind | Examples |
|---|---|
| Nature | Weather, rain, drought, floods, storms |
| People | Visions, inspiration, plagues, fertility |
| Land | Raising mountains, moving rivers, creating or removing resources |
| Direct acts | Blessing, smiting, guiding a migration |

**Rules for interventions:**

1. Every intervention is logged as a cause, so history stays traceable.
2. A world that has been intervened in is marked as such and kept distinct from an untouched one.
3. People perceive interventions and interpret them through their beliefs: an answered prayer can strengthen a faith; a catastrophe can be read as punishment; silence can breed doubt.

**Prayer feed (adopted).** In god mode, the observer can see what people are praying and sacrificing for, and choose whether to answer.

## 10. The observatory

- **2D map** with zoom from the whole world to regions, settlements and individual people.
- **Causal event log.** Every recorded event names its causes (earlier events and state). Clicking an event walks back through why it happened.
- **Chronicles** (annals, oral histories, later newspapers) may only narrate logged events; nothing is invented by the narration.
- **Timelines, genealogies, population and production charts,** and the spread of languages, beliefs and techniques over the map.

## 11. The ten behaviours (success gates)

Each is judged across many runs, not by matching real history.

| # | Behaviour | How it is tested |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Bands endure.** A foraging band in habitable land persists indefinitely, fluctuating around what the land can support. | Nearly all seeds survive many generations; population stays within a band around carrying capacity. |
| 2 | **Groups spread and drift apart.** Growing groups split, colonise new land, and diverge in language, customs and tools. | Occupied area grows from the origin; cultural distance rises with time and separation. |
| 3 | **Farming is an option, not a destiny.** It appears where domesticable species, climate and population pressure coincide; some regions never adopt it. | Adoption dates vary widely across seeds; some regions stay foraging. |
| 4 | **Surplus creates specialists, hierarchy and property.** No surplus, no specialists. | Specialist share follows surplus; disabling food storage prevents it. |
| 5 | **Scale needs institutions, or it breaks apart.** Without matching institutions, or under stress, societies fragment or collapse; recovery follows. | Oversized polities break up; collapses occur in some runs; recovery is observed. |
| 6 | **Conflict has causes and costs.** War follows competition; conquest builds empires that are hard to hold. | Conflict rises with scarcity and proximity; empire duration falls with overextension. |
| 7 | **Knowledge accumulates, spreads and can be lost.** | Innovation tracks connected population; diffusion follows contact; isolation causes loss. |
| 8 | **The population ceiling holds until it doesn't.** Pre-industrial gains mostly become more people; sustained rising living standards are possible but never guaranteed. | Living standards roughly flat over long pre-industrial periods; escape in some runs only. |
| 9 | **Shared belief binds people, and outlives states.** | Cultural and political boundaries diverge; disabling the belief layer changes cohesion and conflict. |
| 10 | **Same rules, different histories.** Geography matters but doesn't decide everything. | Large variation across seeds; identical starting regions diverge. |

**Standing requirement:** every major transition (farming, state formation, collapse, conversion, invention) is traceable to recorded causes.

## 13. Engineering principles

- **Data plus systems, not version inheritance.** World state is plain data; systems are functions with declared inputs and outputs, run in an explicit order.
- **Separate random streams** for each subsystem, so adding one does not reshuffle the others.
- **Every quantity has a source.** Food, people, materials and money are conserved and accounted for.
- **Append-only event log** with causes; versioned saves; exact replay from a save and seed.
- **Git** for history rather than zipped releases; a short `CLAUDE.md` in the project carries context to future sessions.
- **Behavioural gates over test counts.** Unit tests protect correctness; the ten behaviours decide progress.
- **Lessons from the earlier Civilisation Laboratory build** are kept as reference (its accounting, transit and negative results), not copied wholesale.

## 16. Additions agreed in principle (9 November 2026)

Ben agreed to these on 9 November 2026. Each is PROPOSED in detail and decided when its milestone's specification is written. Each must serve one of the ten behaviours or come with a gate check of its own, and every output must trace to recorded causes.

**Deepening what exists**
1. **People change the land.** Overhunting of large game, forest clearance, soil exhaustion and the salting of irrigated land, each recovering slowly or not at all. They feed back into food, famine, migration and collapse (behaviours 5 and 8). *M4 to M6.*
2. **Diseases from domestic animals.** Outbreaks arise where people live closely with their herds. Immunity differs between peoples, so contact between long-separated peoples can be catastrophic in one direction. This builds on M1's outbreaks. *M4 to M5.*
3. **Inherited traits shaped by selection.** A few traits with simple inheritance, such as digesting milk as an adult among herders and inherited resistance to particular diseases. They spread or fade with the advantage they give, and ancestry is traceable like language. *M5 to M6.*

**The people's own record of themselves**

4. **Chronicles written by the people themselves.** Once writing exists, societies keep their own annals, king lists and inscriptions, in their own evolving language and script. These texts are selective and partisan: rulers' victories are recorded and their defeats omitted. The observer can set them beside the true causal record. *M4 (writing), M5.*
5. **Myths and songs that drift.** Real logged events (floods, migrations, great leaders, wars) become oral tradition and change with each retelling, measurably drifting from the event over centuries, as the language drifts. They are material for M5's religion. *M5.*
6. **Place names.** Rivers, mountains, regions and settlements are named in the local language and renamed by newcomers and conquerors. Older names survive beneath, as in real place-name layers. *M4.*

**Warfare, military technology, intelligence and strategy** (agreed 9 November 2026)

9. **Military technology** as nodes in the technology network, each needing materials, prior knowledge and enough people. Examples: bows; bronze and iron weapons and armour; horses, chariots and cavalry; walls and fortifications, and siege methods; ships and naval warfare; much later, gunpowder. Each changes the strength calculation of a war, spreads by contact and capture, and can be lost. *M4.*
10. **Methods of warfare and logistics.** Raids, pitched battles, sieges, ambushes and guerrilla resistance; tribute, occupation, garrisons and devastation. Armies eat: a campaign draws on real stores, the season matters, and distance limits reach. *M4.*
11. **Reconnaissance and espionage.** Polities hold estimates of their neighbours' strength, not the truth. The estimates are built from traders' reports, scouts and spies (specialists paid from surplus), and they can be wrong. Wars are begun or avoided on beliefs, and the cause chain says so ("its scouts reported half the defenders' real strength"). The display shows estimates as estimates. Kept to neighbours and contacts, for cost. *M4.*
12. **Strategy.** Leaders weigh attacking, defending, allying, paying tribute or waiting, using their estimates and the costs. This is bounded rationality, with leaders' judgement good or poor: understandable rules, not a game-playing AI, so every decision answers "why?". *M4 onwards.*

**Crime, norms and their enforcement** (agreed 9 November 2026)

15. **Norms belong to each culture.** What counts as wrongdoing is set by each people's own norms: marriage rules, sharing, whether raiding outsiders is theft or honour. Norms drift like customs. The simulation records violations against those norms, not one fixed morality, and the observer sees both the people's judgement and what actually happened.
    - **Wrongdoing:** theft from stores and fields; violence within a community and the feuds that follow; cheating on obligations (not sharing, evading tribute); corruption (officials skimming tribute); banditry and piracy where rule is weak; later, smuggling and fraud.
    - **Responses:** gossip, loss of standing, shaming, exile and feud before formal law; compensation, punishment and courts with law and judges; later watchmen, prisons and religious sanction. Each costs something real and has effects: feuds can grow into secession, corruption lowers capacity, and banditry grows as states fail.
    - **Causes:** crime rises with hunger, inequality, weak enforcement and the chance of escaping, all already computed, so every rise answers "why?".
    - **Limit:** sexual violence and the abuse of children are represented only as counts at the level of a society, never as individual cases or narrated detail, while still traceable and still affecting outcomes. Cruelty in punishment is treated the same way.

    *Some in M3 (law and judges); mostly M4 (banditry, piracy, corruption, enforcement) and M5 (religious sanction); fraud and smuggling with M6's markets.*

**Notable people, families and personal ties** (Ben, 9 November 2026: reviewed from a proposal Ben drafted with ChatGPT; Claude's recommendations accepted)

Origins already separates how closely a person is simulated from their social standing. Detail depends on distance from the centres of rule and on office, not on class, and that stays.

16. **Lineages and families** as compact records: founder, the society's descent rule (from its customs: through the father, the mother or both), reputation, debts, lands, and standing alliances and rivalries. Membership is worked out from parentage and the descent rule, not stored pair by pair, and lineages can rise, split, migrate and die out. *M4.*
17. **Personal ties between notable people**, in two kinds only:
    - **Obligation:** favours, gifts and protection owed; patronage and reciprocity.
    - **Enmity:** grievance from person to person.

    Ties are created and changed only by events that already happen (marriage, gifts of food, shared war, contested succession, betrayal of an alliance), between people who actually meet. Each person keeps a short list, and old ties fade. *M4, with religious patronage in M5.*
18. **Reputation, and notables emerging from below.** How widely someone is known, earned in war, ritual, building or feasting, separate from wealth and office. A person can be brought out of a folded group by such an event; their ancestry is then marked as unrecorded and never invented. *M4.*
19. **Personal ties must not dominate history.** A knock-out experiment in the M4 gate runs histories with personal ties switched off. Ties should change individual histories without changing the overall pattern of results.

Deferred or rejected, with reasons:
- **Five kinds of relationship** (affection, trust, respect, resentment, fear): simplified to two, because there is no evidence to tune five against.
- **Personality traits:** deferred. Modern personality models do not transfer well across cultures (the Big Five did not replicate among the Tsimane: Gurven et al. 2013; Henrich, Heine & Norenzayan 2010). At most one or two dispositions, such as boldness, inherited and shaped by experience, and only if a gate shows they matter (M6 at the earliest).
- **A separate social-encounter engine:** rejected. Existing events provide the meetings.
- **An upper- and lower-class split:** rejected. It is unnecessary, because detail is already independent of class.

**Watching changes nothing** (Ben, 9 November 2026, option b; built). Following a band only chooses what the observatory shows and how fast time passes. A folded group is shown as a group, and which bands are followed person by person depends only on the world: those farthest from the seats of chiefdoms and states fold first. People brought out of groups by the simulation (for example, a new seat) are marked "ancestry unrecorded".

**Observer tools** (they change nothing in the simulation)

20. **Branching.** From any save, change one thing (a drought, a leader surviving a battle) and run both futures side by side. This is the direct test of behaviour 10, and a natural use of god mode. *Any time after M3.*
21. **History scrubber.** Rewind and replay any span on the map from the saves. *Any time after M3.*
22. **A local runner in Claude Code, in dialogue with this chat** (agreed 12 November 2026).
    - **What it does:** Claude Code on Ben's laptop runs batches of local tests natively, 4–6 at once on its 12 threads.
    - **The channel is the GitHub repository:**
      - this chat writes requests to `local/requests/`;
      - Claude Code runs them and commits results and a short summary to `local/results/`;
      - both append one line per request and result to `docs/LOCAL_LOG.md`.
    - **Same rules on both sides:** both follow `CLAUDE.md`.
    - **Division of work:**
      - this chat keeps design, specifications, gate runs and reports, the literature work, and the checks that run while Ben is away;
      - Claude Code does fast experiments and anything that needs the laptop.
    - **Load:** at most about 4 tests at a time, so the laptop stays usable.
    - *To set up after the G3 gate.* A handover note will come first.

**Considered and left out:** detailed inner lives and personalities for individuals. Inventing them would narrate more than the simulation computes, against the principle that every answer comes from recorded causes.

**Display (agreed 9 November 2026):** smooth movement on the map (built). The final build may draw with WebGL, using the graphics card in the browser: shaded terrain, natural coastlines and rivers, borders that grow and shrink, and animated migrations and trade. It must show only what the simulation computed.

**Computing (agreed 9 November 2026):** an optional graphics-card backend (CuPy) for heavy array work, as a "fast mode" that is statistically, not digit-for-digit, the same. Exact replay and gates stay on the processor. To be proposed with measurements when array work dominates, likely after M4. History records move to an on-disk store by M4.
